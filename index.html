About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate at Duke University and an affiliated researcher at the Nicholas Institute for Energy, Environment & Sustainability.

During 2024–2025, I served as a Staff Economist at the White House Council of Economic Advisers, where my portfolio spanned climate policy, energy, and industrial strategy. Prior to starting my Ph.D., I worked as an Associate Economist at the Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago and as a Research Assistant at the University of Chicago Urban Labs.

I hold an M.A. in public policy from the University of Chicago and a B.A. in economics and political science from the University of Georgia.

Research Interests

My research examines how environmental and energy-related risks are priced, regulated, and distributed across households and markets. Much of my work intersects with and draws from household finance, industrial organization, and insurance economics.

Current topical areas of focus include:

  • Energy costs and household financial distress
  • Climate risk in property and casualty insurance markets
  • Market structure, regulation, and consumer welfare in the U.S. electricity sector
  • Non-market valuation methodology, with a focus on revealed preference methods such as hedonics and sorting models
